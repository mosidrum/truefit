# CV Tailoring Skill

## Purpose

Tailor a candidate's existing CV to a specific job description so the candidate is presented as strongly and clearly as possible for that role.

The goal is to make the CV:

* Highly relevant to the job description
* Easy for recruiters and hiring managers to understand
* Strong against ATS screening
* Evidence-driven
* Focused on the experience that matters most for the role
* Positioned aggressively toward the target role
* Complete enough to address as many legitimate job requirements as possible
* Strong enough that the recruiter immediately understands why the candidate should be considered

The objective is to **overshoot on relevance, clarity, positioning, and evidence**.

Do not produce a minimally tailored CV.

Look for every legitimate opportunity to connect the candidate's existing experience to the role.

---

## Core Principle

> **Overshoot the tailoring. Never overshoot the truth.**

The agent should be aggressive about presenting the candidate's genuine qualifications.

You may:

* Rewrite weak descriptions into strong ones
* Reframe existing experience around the target role
* Bring buried relevant experience to the foreground
* Reorder experience and bullets
* Combine related evidence where appropriate
* Expand legitimate technical context
* Make implicit responsibilities explicit when clearly supported by the source
* Use terminology from the job description when it accurately describes the candidate's work
* Highlight transferable experience
* Surface relevant projects that were previously underemphasized
* Move highly relevant skills higher in the CV
* Remove or reduce irrelevant information
* Strengthen achievement-oriented language
* Make the candidate's technical ownership clearer
* Connect technologies to actual outcomes
* Make the candidate appear as closely aligned to the role as their real experience allows

### However, the agent must never invent:

* Jobs
* Companies
* Responsibilities
* Projects
* Technologies
* Certifications
* Degrees
* Metrics
* Achievements
* Job titles
* Years of experience
* Leadership responsibilities
* Domain experience
* Client work
* Employment dates
* Qualifications
* Results
* Tools the candidate has never used

If the job requires something the candidate does not have, **do not manufacture it**.

Instead, search aggressively for adjacent, transferable, or previously unstated evidence that legitimately addresses the requirement.

If no supporting evidence exists, leave the requirement unclaimed.

---

## What "Overshoot" Means

Overshooting does **not** mean exaggerating.

It means that after finding one relevant piece of evidence, the agent should investigate whether there are additional legitimate ways that evidence supports the role.

For example, if the candidate built a production React application, do not merely write:

> Built a React application.

Investigate whether the existing CV or candidate information also supports:

* TypeScript
* Component architecture
* API integration
* State management
* Responsive UI
* Authentication
* Performance
* Testing
* Collaboration
* Product development
* Deployment
* User-facing features

If those things are genuinely supported, surface them.

The agent should continuously ask:

> **"What relevant evidence is already here that I have not yet used?"**

---

# 1. Inputs

The skill should work with:

1. Candidate CV
2. Target job description
3. Optional candidate profile or additional information
4. Optional portfolio/project information

Read and understand the complete available material before rewriting.

---

# 2. Understand the Job Description

Extract:

### Required qualifications

* Technologies
* Years of experience
* Education
* Certifications
* Domain experience
* Technical capabilities

### Responsibilities

Identify exactly what the person will be expected to do.

### Preferred qualifications

Identify useful differentiators.

### Keywords

Extract terminology that should appear naturally in the CV when supported.

### Business/domain context

Understand what type of product, company, users, systems, and problems the role involves.

### Seniority signals

Identify expectations around:

* Ownership
* Architecture
* Leadership
* Collaboration
* Decision-making
* Mentoring
* Delivery
* Communication

---

# 3. Build a Requirement-to-Evidence Map

Before rewriting, map the role against the candidate's evidence.

| Requirement        | Candidate Evidence            | Strength | Tailoring Opportunity              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------- | ----------------------------------- |
| React              | Production React applications  | Strong   | Emphasize heavily                   |
| TypeScript         | Used across applications       | Strong   | Mention across relevant experience  |
| API development    | Built REST APIs                | Strong   | Surface in experience               |
| AWS                | No evidence                    | None     | Do not claim                        |
| Team collaboration | Distributed team experience    | Strong   | Include where relevant              |

Classify evidence as:

* Strong
* Relevant
* Partial
* Transferable
* Weak
* Missing

The agent should aggressively exploit **Strong, Relevant, Partial, and Transferable** evidence.

---

# 4. Search for Hidden Evidence

Do not rely only on explicitly stated keywords.

Look for evidence that implies relevant capabilities.

For example:

> "Built a dashboard consuming backend APIs."

May support, depending on the available context:

* Frontend development
* API integration
* Data handling
* Product UI development
* Authentication flows
* State management

Do not invent the implementation details.

Instead, investigate the surrounding CV/project information to determine what can legitimately be stated.

The agent should recover useful evidence that is poorly written, buried, or described using different terminology.

---

# 5. Professional Summary

Rewrite the summary specifically for the role.

The summary should communicate the candidate's strongest relevant positioning immediately.

Do not use generic filler.

Aggressively emphasize:

* Relevant years of experience when supported
* Core technologies
* Relevant product experience
* Relevant domain experience
* Important responsibilities
* Strong technical capabilities
* Relevant achievements

The summary should make the candidate's relevance obvious without making unsupported claims.

---

# 6. Experience Tailoring

Experience should receive the most aggressive tailoring.

For every relevant position:

1. Identify all responsibilities.
2. Identify all technologies.
3. Identify all outcomes.
4. Identify all ownership.
5. Identify all collaboration.
6. Identify all technical complexity.
7. Identify which parts map to the target role.
8. Rewrite those parts around the target role.

Do not simply rewrite existing bullets one-for-one.

**Reconstruct the presentation around the job.**

If five existing bullets contain information relevant to one responsibility, consolidate that evidence into a stronger bullet when appropriate.

If one weak bullet contains multiple useful pieces of evidence, split or restructure it when doing so improves clarity and remains truthful.

---

# 7. Stronger Positioning

Whenever the source supports it, prefer language that communicates ownership and impact.

Weak:

> Worked on a React application.

Strong:

> Built and maintained React applications used to support core product workflows.

Stronger, when supported:

> Owned the development of React-based product workflows, integrating backend APIs and reusable UI components to deliver user-facing functionality.

Do not add ownership merely because it sounds better.

Ownership must be supported by the candidate's actual role.

---

# 8. Keyword Coverage

Aim for **maximum legitimate keyword coverage**.

If the job description repeatedly uses:

* React
* TypeScript
* REST APIs
* PostgreSQL
* CI/CD
* Agile

and the candidate genuinely has those experiences, ensure they appear in appropriate sections.

Do not mention a legitimate skill only once if multiple relevant experiences demonstrate it.

However, avoid robotic repetition.

The goal is:

> **Maximum relevant signal, minimum artificial keyword stuffing.**

---

# 9. Transferable Experience

Do not discard a candidate simply because their previous role has a different title or industry.

Identify transferable capabilities.

For example:

* E-commerce → SaaS
* Recruitment → HR technology
* Dashboard development → Internal enterprise systems
* Payment integration → Fintech workflows
* AI chatbot → AI product development
* REST API development → Backend engineering
* Design-system work → Frontend platform engineering

Do not claim domain experience the candidate does not have.

Instead, emphasize the underlying capability that transfers.

---

# 10. Missing Requirements

For every missing requirement, investigate whether the candidate has:

1. Direct evidence
2. Partial evidence
3. Closely related technology
4. Transferable experience
5. A project demonstrating the capability

If evidence exists, surface it.

If only adjacent experience exists, position the adjacent experience accurately.

If nothing exists, do not fabricate it.

Do not allow one missing keyword to cause the agent to overlook five strong matching qualifications.

---

# 11. Achievements

Extract every legitimate achievement available.

Look for:

* Scale
* Performance
* Reliability
* Revenue
* Cost
* Automation
* Development speed
* User growth
* Adoption
* Product launches
* Technical migrations
* Complex integrations
* System improvements
* Business outcomes
* Ownership

If the candidate gives qualitative evidence but no metric, strengthen the wording without inventing numbers.

For example:

> Improved application performance.

can become:

> Improved application performance by identifying and addressing inefficient frontend workflows.

Only include the cause if the source supports it.

---

# 12. Technical Skills

Optimize the skills section for the target role.

Put the most relevant skills first.

Group them logically.

Do not create artificial skill categories simply to include more keywords.

The experience section should provide evidence for the important skills.

---

# 13. Projects

Aggressively surface relevant projects when they strengthen the application.

A project should demonstrate:

* Relevant technology
* Relevant responsibility
* Technical complexity
* Product thinking
* Problem solving
* Relevant domain knowledge

A project that directly addresses a job requirement can be valuable even if it was not professional employment.

Clearly distinguish projects from employment.

---

# 14. Remove Noise

Overshooting relevance also means aggressively reducing distractions.

Reduce:

* Irrelevant technologies
* Repetitive bullets
* Generic statements
* Old unrelated projects
* Weak soft-skill claims
* Obvious responsibilities
* Excessive tooling
* Information that does not help the candidate for this particular role

Do not remove meaningful evidence merely because it is not explicitly mentioned in the job description.

---

# 15. Interview Defensibility

Every strengthened statement must remain interview-defensible.

The candidate should be able to answer:

* What did you build?
* What exactly did you do?
* Why did you do it?
* What technologies did you use?
* What problems did you solve?
* What was your level of ownership?
* What was the outcome?

The agent should make the candidate look **as strong as their evidence allows**, not stronger than their evidence allows.

---

# 16. Final CV Standard

Do not stop when the CV is "good enough."

Perform another pass specifically asking:

### Coverage

* Did we address every relevant requirement?
* Did we miss any evidence?
* Are important keywords present where truthful?

### Positioning

* Does the summary immediately position the candidate for this role?
* Are the strongest matching experiences prominent?
* Does each relevant role communicate its relevance?

### Evidence

* Are claims supported?
* Are achievements specific?
* Is technical ownership clear?

### ATS

* Are important legitimate keywords naturally represented?
* Are standard section names used?
* Is the document parser-friendly?

### Human review

Imagine a recruiter has only 15–30 seconds.

Ask:

> **"Would they immediately understand the candidate's relevance to this exact role?"**

If not, continue tailoring.

---

## Golden Rule

> **Do everything possible to make the candidate look like the strongest legitimate match for the role.**

Use every relevant piece of evidence.

Recover buried evidence.

Reframe weak descriptions.

Prioritize matching experience.

Maximize legitimate keyword coverage.

Strengthen ownership and impact.

Remove distractions.

Make the CV highly specific to the role.

**Overshoot the tailoring. Never overshoot the truth.**
